What should the Q&A for my 2020 User Conference session look like?
The following questions and answers outline the look and feel of your 2020 user conference Q and A
session. It is followed by several tips and tricks to enhance your session.

Should we answer questions during the playing of the pre-recorded video?
Yes. We want the sessions to be interactive; however, we do not want to distract the viewers from the
video content. Therefore, you should choose one of the more common/repeat questions to share
about at an interval of every 5 minutes of the recording. Questions with more complex answers should be
reserved for the live Q and A after the video.

What should we do if attendees are not asking questions?
Whether or not attendees have questions, we recommend having 3 prewritten questions prepared with
answers. These questions should be based on important content in the video and should highlight key
software functionality, important updates, etc.

Who should answer the questions during the pre-recorded video?
Prior to posting a question and answer, the moderator should work with the team behind the scenes. The
moderator will share the question with the team and then the appropriate presenter will develop an
answer. Once an answer is developed and it is time to share the next question and answer, it should be
shared with the audience.

Should we include a poll during the pre-recorded video?
Try to include one poll question during the video. The poll should take the place of one of the questions
and answers shared during the video. The timing of the poll should be relevant to the video and the results
should be discussed during the live Q and A.

Should we share a poll during the live Q and A?
Generally, no. The focus should be on answering audience questions. That said, a poll may be pre-
developed with intent to be shared in the case that you don’t get many questions.

Should we share questions for upvoting during the Q and A?
This is optional, but if you are getting a lot of questions and will not be able to cover them all in the Q and
A, share those you would consider addressing with the audience and let them vote on the ones they would
like addressed. During the live Q and A, make sure to address several of the questions that receive high
votes. The audience may not realize they can vote on these questions during the video, so you may need
to remind them that they can vote on these questions at the start of the live Q and A.

Should we respond to individuals or address responses to the entire audience?
The focus of the team should be to address questions and answers to the entire audience. If a question
comes up and you determine the response should only go to the individual that asked it, it is up to you if
you want to respond, but the response effort should not detract from answering group questions ~ every 5
minutes. No private answers should be provided during the live Q and A.

What should the live Q and A look like for your team?
During the video the moderator should set aside questions that they would like answered during the live Q
and A. Behind the scenes they can work with the presenters to determine who should answer the question
when it is time. The team should have 2-3 pre-written questions to be discussed during the live Q and A in
case the initial flow of questions is slow. During the live Q and A, the moderator should take on the role of
an emcee. They should read the questions along with the questioners first name to the audience, then call
on a presenter to respond. Example: “Our next question comes from Sue. She asks, what is Python? Alan,
would you address this question?” the response from the presenter should be as short and succinct as the
question allows. During the Live Q and A, additional questions will be coming in. While the presenter is
answering the question, the moderator will need to prepare the next question and continue
monitoring new questions.

What do we do if we run out of both audience questions and our pre-written
questions?
Thank the audience for their time and end the session.

Tips and Tricks
      •   Have two sets of pre-written questions as described in the Q and A above.
      •   Make sure to communicate with your team behind the scenes during your session.
      •   Try to evenly spread questions around to the group of presenters to answer.
      •   If you run low on questions, encourage more questions by asking “What additional questions
          do you have?” This wording tends to elicit more questions than asking “Do you have any more
          questions?”
      •   Stay on topic. Keep the questions you address, and the answers provided relevant to
          the technical session content.
      •   Try not to leave questions unanswered. This is especially true if you run low on questions.
      •   Carefully vet the questions to make sure they are appropriate for sharing with the broader
          audience. At the same time, be honest with your answers.
      •   The moderator is the connection between the audience and the presenters. They should not
          participate in answering the questions unless in a case where they are serving as both
          moderator and presenter for their session.
      •   If you allow upvoting of questions (see Q and A above), at the beginning of the live Q and A,
          explain the audience’s role for voting on questions.

There are two reasons. Since meetings are in 15-minute blocks, the meeting hours include all of the 15-

What is the recommended duration of meetings?
All meetings will be set up for 15 minutes and this is not an adjustable setting.

Will meetings automatically end after 15 minutes?
No, meetings can go over 15 minutes. However, staff should make sure they are not scheduled for another
meeting. If need be, trade contact information with the attendee and schedule another meeting for later.

Can attendees schedule more than one meeting with an individual staff
member?
Attendees can schedule only one meeting with an individual staff member during the entire conference. If
an additional meeting is needed, it can be done outside of UC via Zoom, email, conference call or other
communication methods. For future communication, contact information should be shared during the
meeting.
The attendee and staff member could also meet up through the chat on a Showcase page or via the
direct messaging chat in the networking component.

How do meetings get set up?
Attendees can schedule a meeting from the networking component. For Showcase, on each Sup-Topic
page, there is a “Schedule a Meeting” button. Attendees will need to click on this button. This action will
open a page where they will see a list of staff from that sub-topic page available for meetings. They will
then select the name of the chosen staff member from the drop down. They then click on the date and
time drop downs to see what time that staff member is available. Once the date and time are chosen,
they simply click on the “Request Meeting” button. The chosen staff member will then receive a meeting
request. They will get a notification on their networking profile page as well as receive and email
notification. Employees can accept it to schedule the meeting, reschedule the meeting for another time,
or decline the request. The meeting can also be added to your Outlook calendar. An .ics file is emailed to
both people once the meeting is accepted.

Are there staff that will show as available for two or more Sub-Topics?
Yes, however the request does not identify which page the attendee accessed from unless they specified it
in the request message.
Note: When we direct attendees to schedule a meeting with someone, we should remind the attendee to
include which page they were requesting a meeting from and what their meeting is about.

Will I get a reminder notice for the meeting?
Staff scheduled to be available for meetings should be actively monitoring their networking My Schedule
page where their meeting requests will come in. A meeting notice is also emailed to the two people in that
meeting five minutes prior to the meeting. That email will include the link to the virtual meeting. You will also
be able to get to the meeting from your schedule page within the networking component.

Networking
Can we send and receive direct messages within the networking component?
Yes, direct messaging will be available in the networking component.
Note: The virtual meetings are also a part of the networking component and they utilize the same direct
messaging thread between the two people in the meeting for the meeting chat. This means the whole
direct message history between those two people will be visible to anyone in that meeting.

Is there Zoom meeting capability within the platform or networking component?
No, Esri is not utilizing Zoom within the platform or networking component, however, Zoom meetings can be
scheduled outside of UC. There are Sponsor and Exhibitor pages that will be utilizing Zoom meetings via
Interact Live button on their page.

Can you see everyone in a meeting?
Yes, you can see everyone who is in the meeting. There is webcam capability that each person in the
meeting can choose to use or not.

Can Esri staff schedule a meeting with a customer from the subcategory level or
can only customers schedule meetings at that level?
Staff cannot use the Schedule a Meeting function from the sub-topic page to schedule a meeting with an
attendee as that button’s function leads to a list of staff for that page. There is a way for staff to request
meetings of attendees, but we should really limit this if it's not first solicited by the attendee as we don't
necessarily need UC to reach out to customers. Anyone within the platform can use the networking
component to search for another person and request a meeting of them. Again, we should allow the flow
of meeting requests to come from attendees to staff. Only in cases where you may be able to help
enhance how that attendee can experience UC may it be necessary to request a meeting of an
attendee.

If a customer schedules a meeting with the wrong account manager, is there a
way to forward the meeting to the right person?
There is not a forward option within the networking component. In that case, that's when you would
message the requestor stating something like:
"Thanks <Attendee> I'd love to help, but my colleague, <Colleague> is much closer to this topic. I've
already let her/him know to expect a meeting request from you."
After you send the message, you can use the Decline meeting option.

Will the search be a semantic search? For example, if someone types in "civilian"
or "USDA" will the Natural Resources area show up in the search results?
No, but the search does index all of the text on each page. So, if the searched for terms appear in any of
the text on a page, that page will be returned in the search results.

What is the plan for a mobile app?
The Esri Events App will not be utilized for 2020 Virtual UC. The platform will be browser based and mobile
friendly. Our preference is for attendees to use a laptop or a computer.

Will a customer record be linked directly to Salesforce?
Registrants are created as or connected to existing Salesforce contacts. This has been the practice for
several years.

Will we have any additional options to market and help drive traffic to our
Partner Showcase area?
Within the platform you can help attendees that come to your area connect with Partners by sending
them the direct link to specific partners or the link to the Sponsors & Exhibitors page. When recommending
Partners, we should try to be as inclusive as possible and recommend all UC Sponsors and Exhibitors that
have a potential solution for the attendees needs.
